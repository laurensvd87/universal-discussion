# Brand direction screen — 2026-10-04

Status: exploratory, not a name decision or trademark clearance.

## Product promise before a name

The distinctive experience is **one conversation following a topic across
different pages**, with useful, source-aware insights available before a
community is large. It is not primarily a messenger, a comment box for each
URL, or an AI-chat product. A concise English positioning line to test is:
"The conversation follows the topic, not the link."

A name should be easy to say in English, German and Dutch, easy to spell after
hearing once, short enough for a browser icon/app launcher, and friendly
without sounding unserious beside news or critical product discussion. A
subtle connection/perspective cue is useful, but a forced "Venn" pun or an
"AI" suffix is not required. The name need not explain the product alone;
the positioning line and first-run experience can do that.

## Preliminary collision and fit findings

- **Jibber** has the conversational energy the owner likes, but an existing
  [Jibber Social iOS app](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/jibber-social/id1602024272)
  makes the exact name a poor candidate. It also implies ordinary chat more
  strongly than cross-page topic discovery. Phonetic near-clones would need
  careful review, not just a spelling change.
- **Vennture** is used by a [community/AI discovery product](https://vennture.com/about/).
  **Havenn** is used with the same Haven-plus-Venn naming explanation by
  [Havenn Homes](https://havennhomes.com/about-us). Neither is a strong
  ownable direction for this product.
- **Threadle** is already a [Chrome extension for commenting on any website](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/threadle/adjjhbdpnkjhgdfeoohjinbiaicjelhi).
  This is a particularly close naming/positioning collision, even though its
  public description is page-thread-first rather than our semantic-Topic model.
- Short coined names are heavily occupied; a quick web search is useful to
  reject obvious collisions, but silence in search results is not legal,
  store-name or domain clearance. No checked candidate yet has both clear
  product fit and a sufficiently clean preliminary screen to justify a rebrand.

An additional exploratory round found exact product/app uses for ten modern-
sounding candidates: Vaylo, Sidera, Nuvra, Tembra, Avello, Viora, Pendra,
Lunelo, Sovelo and Relayo. Especially relevant collisions include the
[Nuvra social app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nuvra.app),
[Viora social app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.viora.media.meet)
and [Sovelo conversation-practice app](https://sovelo.app/). None is a
recommended shortlist entry. This is a web/store collision screen, not a
trademark, domain or cross-jurisdiction clearance search.

Two unselected directions for a later pronunciation/recall test are
**Topicfold** (many pages folded into one Topic, but more utility-sounding)
and **Crossay** (short and conversational, but ambiguous to hear/spell and
also a French surname). A quick exact-name web/app search did not establish
either as clear; absence of a surfaced app is not availability. Neither
should drive a UI or logo rebrand yet.

## Recommendation

Keep the working product name while testing the core experience. Develop a
small, genuinely distinct shortlist around **shared perspective / connection
across pages**, with the light, memorable rhythm of "Jibber" but enough
credibility for serious discussion. Screen shortlisted spellings in the
Chrome Web Store, Apple App Store, Google Play, web/domain results and relevant
trademark databases before choosing one. Do not redesign the logo or dynamic
background around an unselected name.

For visual exploration, a restrained meeting-of-perspectives motif (two
subtle lines or shapes converging when a match appears) maps to the product
better than always-moving chat bubbles. Motion must not impair reading and
must honor reduced-motion settings. This is a design direction, not an
implementation or release decision.
